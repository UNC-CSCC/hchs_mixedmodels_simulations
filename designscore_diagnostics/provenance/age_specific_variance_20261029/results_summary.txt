Age-specific subject random-intercept variance check — 29 October 2026

Each saved complete population contains 1,266,981 subjects and 633,602
households, with three visits per subject. Younger means baseline age <45;
older means baseline age >=45. There are 773,457 younger and 493,524 older
subjects in each population. No visit-missingness mask was applied.

Primary model: unweighted Gaussian maximum likelihood, using the same fixed
effects as scenario 3 (intercept, age group, x12, x17, x18, x6, y_bmi), one
shared household random-intercept variance, separate subject random-intercept
variances by age group, and separate independent visit-residual variances by
age group. Fixed effects are jointly reestimated, not held at saved values.
Households containing both age groups retain their shared household effect.
No block-group random effect is included, matching the scenario 3 working model.

INTERPRETATION
Subject random-intercept variance is essentially equal across age groups in
Dec2024, while the fitted variance is appreciably greater among older subjects
in Oct2026. This difference persists after allowing age-specific visit noise.
It supports the proposed interaction between age-dependent variability,
unequal age-group sampling, and scenario 3's within-household weight scaling.
It does not establish what fraction of the original scenario 3 bias is caused
by this mechanism. These are fitted variance components under an expanded
scenario 3 working model, rather than identified latent generating variances.
No superpopulation hypothesis test is reported for these census contrasts.

VERIFICATION AND COMPUTATION
- Exact Gaussian likelihood, compressed by household age composition using
  subject means and within-subject contrasts; no approximation to the Gaussian
  covariance likelihood and no sampled population subset for the final fits.
- Direct dense covariance calculations independently reproduce the likelihood
  and profiled coefficients on observed-data subsets of both populations.
- Analytic derivatives agree with numerical derivatives on those subsets.
- Independent optimizer starts are polished to zero analytic scores and agree
  to within the programmed relative tolerance of 1e-6.
- A common-variance fit reproduces the saved Stata census variances to a maximum
  relative difference of 0.0025%, and fixed effects within 0.000002.
- Python and R were limited to one logical CPU at below-normal priority.
- Existing population files were read only. No Stata process was launched,
  stopped, suspended, or reconfigured. No MI source or output was edited.
